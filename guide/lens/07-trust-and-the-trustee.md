# Trust, and the trustee

## The idea

A **trust** splits a thing three ways, among three parties:

- the **grantor**, who puts the thing in and sets the terms;
- the **trustee**, who holds the title ([step 4](04-title-is-all-that-is-sold.md)) and must manage it by those terms: a duty, not a benefit;
- the **beneficiary**, who has the benefit.

Scott Duncan reads a corporation exactly this way: its founders are the grantors, its directors (who hold titles) are trustees, and its shareholders, the equity holders of [step 3](03-what-is-had-and-what-is-owed.md), are the beneficiaries. And anyone acting for another, an **agent**, is in his words always a trustee, with the duties that brings.

In the lens, a debt is the record of a trust not kept: something was held or promised for another, and it wasn't delivered.

## In the lemonade stand

If Mia's stand were a corporation: Mia sets it up (grantor); a director runs it and holds what it has in its name (trustee); the shareholders, Mia included, have the benefit of what it earns (beneficiaries).

## In their words

> "When you create a LIMITED LIABILITY CORPORATION, you and the Governmaent are acting as GRANTORS - This ends upon creation and the GRANTORS have no further say. 1: A coprpration has SHAREHOLDERS who are the BENEFICIARIES. … 2: A corporation has DIRECTORS with titles (TRUSTEES), and you can be one."
> — [Scott Duncan, Volume 1, 9 March 2013](https://thetenderforlaw.com/vol_i/462493170453287_485784274790843/#entry-490499764319294)

> "There are three PARTIES in a TRUST"
> — [Scott Duncan, Obiter Dictum: Meads v. Meads for Idiots](https://thetenderforlaw.com/articles/scott-duncan/obiter-dictum-meades-v-meads-for-idiots/)

> "AN AGENT IS ALWAYS A TRUSTEE! ALWAYS!"
> — [Scott Duncan, Volume 8, 12 December 2014](https://thetenderforlaw.com/vol_viii/462493170453287_756770651025536/#entry-759182050784396)

> "You REGISTER (transfer title) your VEHICLE, and it becomes Government (Trust) property. You still OWN it, but it is FIDUCIARY OWNERSHIP. You have duties and obligations to fufill in order to "own" the vehicle."
> — [Scott Duncan, Volume 4, 21 October 2013](https://thetenderforlaw.com/vol_iv/462493170453287_573236309378972/#entry-576416412394295)

> "If you have DEBT with me, it means you breached a trust"
> — [Scott Duncan, Volume 6, 5 May 2015](https://thetenderforlaw.com/vol_vi/462493170453287_683006771735258/#entry-826140964088504)

> "A trust cannot have debt; and from a bookkeeping perspective the TRUSTEES owe me substantially more than I owe them."
> — [Scott Duncan, Volume 5, 29 January 2014](https://thetenderforlaw.com/vol_v/462493170453287_618004378235498/)
