# Surety: the responsible party

## The idea

The books record what is owed. **Surety** answers the next question: *who must pay it?* In ordinary usage a surety is someone who promises to answer for another's debt. Scott Duncan uses the word for the responsible party in general, and makes it the second half of everything legal: LEGAL = SURETY AND ACCOUNTING.

Read through this lens, much of the archive is about where surety sits: who took it on (often without noticing), and how it can be limited or passed to someone else. A limited company is the plainest example: the letters LTD. announce that the shareholders' liability is limited.

## In the lemonade stand

Mom lends the stand $30. The stand owes it: a liability in its books. If the stand can't pay, who answers for it? If Mia had signed a promise to repay Mom herself, she would be surety for the stand's debt, and her own savings would be on the line, not only her stake.

## In their words

> "LEGALLY the only consideration is who must pay. … LEGAL = Accounting and Surety … Surety simply means "responsible party"."
> — [Scott Duncan, Volume 5, 1 January 2014](https://thetenderforlaw.com/vol_v/462493170453287_606995862669683/)

> "You assume ALL LIABILITY when you ask to join this group. This, by default, means you have "SURETY", because SURETY equals LIABILITY."
> — [Scott Duncan, Volume 1, 10 March 2013](https://thetenderforlaw.com/vol_i/462493170453287_490608044308466/)

> "In the end it all refers to surety and accounting -- nothing else. In order to completely remove surety (insure), another PERSON must take responsibility (SURETY) for your actions."
> — [Scott Duncan, Volume 3, 11 July 2013](https://thetenderforlaw.com/vol_iii/462493170453287_535384496497487/)

> "Liability doesn't magically appear. It's why LTD. is written after corporate names. LIABILITY IS LIMITED."
> — [Scott Duncan, Volume 6, 25 May 2014](https://thetenderforlaw.com/vol_vi/462493170453287_671761712859764/#entry-672185976150671)

## Check yourself

> [!question]- A corporation with LTD. after its name owes $10,000 it can't pay. In this lens, what does "LTD." tell you about who must pay?
> That the shareholders' liability is limited: the corporation is the responsible party for its debts, and its shareholders risk only their stake, unless one of them took on surety for it, for instance by personally guaranteeing the debt.
