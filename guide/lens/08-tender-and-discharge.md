## The idea

The archive takes its name from this word. To **tender** is to present an offer for acceptance. Scott Duncan reads the words on a bank note, THIS NOTE IS LEGAL TENDER, through that definition: the note is an offer, and whoever uses it accepts the terms that come with it. That is the group's name: THE TENDER FOR LAW.

To **discharge** is the accounting end of a debt: the entry that brings an item's balance to zero. Chris Schulte shows the choice from [step 3](03-what-is-had-and-what-is-owed.md) at work here: credit a liability account and the payments discharge it, building the corporation's credit.

With this, the lens is complete: books with two sides (2), what is had and owed (3), title (4), the responsible party (5), money as a debt (6), trust (7), and the offer and the settlement (8). The volumes can now be read as entries.

## In their words

> "TENDER in the context of Law means, "to present to PERSON, an unconditional offer, to enter into a contract". Look at those words, then look at a bank note. THIS NOTE IS LEGAL TENDER means, "THIS NOTE IS AN UNCONDITIONAL OFFER TO ENTER INTO A CONTRACT FOR LAW"."
> — [Scott Duncan, Legalese for Idiots](https://thetenderforlaw.com/articles/scott-duncan/legalese-for-idiots/)

> "THIS NOTE IS LEGAL TENDER=THIS NOTE IS A TENDER FOR LAW … LEGAL TENDER is NOT MONEY, NOR is it CURRENCY. It is ATTACHED to money/currency."
> — [Scott Duncan, Volume 6, 24 June 2014](https://thetenderforlaw.com/vol_vi/462493170453287_685294971506438/)

> "USING LEGAL TENDER binds you. Using MONEY does not."
> — [Scott Duncan, Volume 10, 3 July 2015](https://thetenderforlaw.com/vol_x/462493170453287_847918695244064/#entry-849675381735062)

> "REMITTANCE - Advice to a SECURED PARTY on how to DISCHARGE the debt. DISCHARGE - to "zero-out" a balance of an item on an account."
> — [Scott Duncan, Volume 3, 4 July 2013](https://thetenderforlaw.com/vol_iii/462493170453287_532937380075532/#entry-532943700074900)

> "By crediting a liability account, you can discharge a monthly payment and build credit."
> — [Chris Schulte, An Article About Accounting](https://thetenderforlaw.com/articles/chris-schulte/an-article-about-accounting/)

## Check yourself

> [!question]- Scott Duncan defines DISCHARGE as "to zero-out a balance of an item on an account". In the books, what does a discharge of a $50 debt look like?
> An entry that brings the debt's balance from $50 to zero: in the debtor's books, the liability is debited $50 (it goes down on the left), and the other side records what settled it.
