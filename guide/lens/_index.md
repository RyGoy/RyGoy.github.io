# Path 1: The accounting lens

Everything in THE TENDER FOR LAW is to be read through an accounting lens: who owes what to whom, and who is responsible for it. This path builds that way of seeing from nothing, one idea on top of the last. Take the steps in order; each takes ten minutes or so. THE ACCOUNTING NEVER CHANGES.

| Step | Lesson | You'll be able to |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | [It all comes down to accounting](01-it-all-comes-down-to-accounting.md) | Say why the archive reads everything "legal" as surety and accounting |
| 2 | [Every entry has two sides](02-every-entry-has-two-sides.md) | Write an event as a debit and a credit, and ask whose books you're reading |
| 3 | [What is had, and what is owed](03-what-is-had-and-what-is-owed.md) | Tell an asset from a liability from equity, and why the last two share a side |
| 4 | [Title is all that is sold](04-title-is-all-that-is-sold.md) | Separate title, use and a claim |
| 5 | [Surety: the responsible party](05-surety-the-responsible-party.md) | Find who must pay |
| 6 | [Money is debt in transit](06-money-is-debt-in-transit.md) | See a bank note as one side of a debt |
| 7 | [Trust, and the trustee](07-trust-and-the-trustee.md) | Name the three parties to a trust, and read a corporation as one |
| 8 | [Tender, and discharge](08-tender-and-discharge.md) | Say what "legal tender" offers, and what discharging a debt does in the books |

**Before you start:** [Start Here: Debits and Credits](https://thetenderforlaw.com/accounting/start-here/) on the site (five minutes), and the first deck of the [flash cards](https://thetenderforlaw.com/accounting/flashcards/), Mia's Lemonade Stand. Steps 2 and 3 build on both.

## Questions the archive never answers

Scott Duncan answered the questions he was asked, and nobody asked a beginner's questions. Writing this path showed where the archive is silent. Each of these is a good question for the moderators through "Ask a question" on the site; their answers are published as commentary, and this guide can then quote them.

1. **The archive's dictionary has no entry (yet) for Debit, Asset, Equity, Fiduciary or Capital**, the words the lens rests on. The lessons explain them in plain accounting terms for now.
2. **Scott Duncan's three parties to a trust** are named in his reading of a corporation (grantors, trustees, beneficiaries: [step 7](07-trust-and-the-trustee.md)), but not in his article on trusts. Are these the three he means everywhere?
3. **Money of exchange and money of account** ([step 6](06-money-is-debt-in-transit.md)): which side of whose books is each on?
4. **"Using legal tender binds you. Using money does not."** ([step 8](08-tender-and-discharge.md)): what, in the books, is the difference between using one and the other?
