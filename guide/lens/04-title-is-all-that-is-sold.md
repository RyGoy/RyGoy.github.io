## The idea

Everyday speech says you "own" your car, your house, your company. The lens separates three things that speech runs together:

- **Title**: the record of whose name a thing is held in. Title is what is bought and sold.
- **Having the use of it**: you can drive the car, live in the house.
- **A claim on it**: as a lender, or as an equity holder ([step 3](03-what-is-had-and-what-is-owed.md)).

Scott Duncan's point is that registering something moves its title. A shareholder doesn't *own* a corporation: he holds a share, a claim. And whoever holds title has duties that come with it ([step 7](07-trust-and-the-trustee.md)).

## In the lemonade stand

Once Mia puts her $20 into the stand's tin, the $20 is the stand's: it is held in the stand's name. Mia cannot own what she has no title to. What she holds is her stake, a claim on the stand. [Start Here](https://thetenderforlaw.com/accounting/start-here/) makes the same point.

## In their words

> "Title is all that is sold."
> — [Scott Duncan, Volume 1, 15 February 2013](https://thetenderforlaw.com/vol_i/462493170453287_481479325221338/#entry-481489625220308)

> ""Property is mere TITLE". You are only a "tennant". You don't OWN it like other property. NOTHING you "register" is owned by you."
> — [Scott Duncan, Volume 1, 8 March 2013](https://thetenderforlaw.com/vol_i/462493170453287_488326607869943/#entry-489908367711767)

> "You also don't OWN a corporation (That would be SLAVERY). You CREATE a corporation, then grant ONE SHARE. You are now the SOLE SHAREHOLDER (not an "owner")."
> — [Scott Duncan, Volume 1, 29 March 2013](https://thetenderforlaw.com/vol_i/462493170453287_497054310330506/#entry-497245080311429)

> "IF YOU LIEN IT, YOU OWN IT. THAT'S WHAT A LIEN IS; OWNERSHIP OF TITLE."
> — [Scott Duncan, Volume 13, 22 February 2016](https://thetenderforlaw.com/vol_xiii/462493170453287_948042318565034/)

## Check yourself

> [!question]- You are the only shareholder of a corporation that holds a truck in its name. Who has title to the truck, and what do you hold?
> The corporation has title: the truck is held in its name. You hold a share: a claim on the corporation, not the truck.
