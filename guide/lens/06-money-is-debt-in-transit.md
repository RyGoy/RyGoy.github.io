# Money is debt in transit

[The accounting lens](_index.md) · step 6 of 8 · You need first: [Surety: the responsible party](05-surety-the-responsible-party.md) · Next: [Trust, and the trustee](07-trust-and-the-trustee.md)

## The idea

Apply the two-sides habit from [step 2](02-every-entry-has-two-sides.md) to a bank note. In your books it is cash: something you have. Now ask whose *other* books it appears in. The Bank of Canada notes are a **liability** to the Bank and an asset to the holder. So the note in your pocket is one side of a debt, and its other side sits in the books of whoever signed it.

In Scott Duncan's words, "[A LEGAL document that bears two signatures and a TRUSTEE, is LEGALLY a SECURITY.](https://thetenderforlaw.com/articles/scott-duncan/lies-and-lying-liars-who-lie-about-the-liaes-they-tell/)" And "[Bank NOTES, STOCKS, BONDS, are LEGAL SECURITIES.](https://thetenderforlaw.com/articles/scott-duncan/lies-and-lying-liars-who-lie-about-the-liaes-they-tell/)"

[**Money**](../terms/money.md) is debt/accounting in transit: "[money is simply DEBT IN TRANSIT](https://thetenderforlaw.com/articles/scott-duncan/lien-your-name/)", and "[Money is based on PROMISES](https://thetenderforlaw.com/vol_xiii/462493170453287_948042318565034/#entry-948089958560270)", passing from hand to hand. The responsible party ([surety](05-surety-the-responsible-party.md)) is the signer, who controls the books and made the **liability** in them. The **holder** has possession of the note, not liability for the underlying debt; they are responsible for this stranger's document until it's no longer in their possession: "[Cash gives you the BENEFIT but no LIABILITY (Your signature is not on it).](https://thetenderforlaw.com/vol_i/462493170453287_462528253783112/#entry-499999823369288)" This is why Scott tells readers to look at the money they hold: "[Notice there are names and signatures on it? NONE OF THEM ARE YOURS.](https://thetenderforlaw.com/vol_xv/462493170453287_1044508052251793/)"

He also separates two kinds of money: [**money of exchange**](../terms/money-of-exchange.md), which buys goods and services (“[MONEY OF EXCHANGE IS able to purchase consumables](https://thetenderforlaw.com/vol_i/462493170453287_462528253783112/#entry-482730195096251)”), and [**money of account**](../terms/money-of-account.md), which exists only as entries in books (“[Money of account is not physical](https://thetenderforlaw.com/vol_viii/462493170453287_736283879740880/#entry-737007556335179)”). Drawing a bill of exchange or making a promise both require a signature; the difference is who's liable/paying. Promises bind the maker and are new accounting: "[Only PROMISSORY NOTES need surety.](https://thetenderforlaw.com/vol_i/462493170453287_480677688634835/#entry-480815961954341)" Orders to pay are directions to a drawee to fulfill the promise, and these orders are based on an existing account/debt between the drawer and the drawee. On a bill, in Scott Duncan's terms, "[PAYEE = BENEFICIARY](https://thetenderforlaw.com/vol_iii/462493170453287_548223475213589/#entry-548291748540095)", "[DRAWER = TRUSTEE](https://thetenderforlaw.com/vol_iii/462493170453287_548223475213589/#entry-548291748540095)" and "[DRAWEE = SURETY](https://thetenderforlaw.com/vol_iii/462493170453287_548223475213589/#entry-548291748540095)"; and "[If it has an ENDORSEMENT (Second Signature) it is a BILL OF EXCHANGE.](https://thetenderforlaw.com/vol_xiii/462493170453287_938965852806014/#entry-938974076138525)"

## In their words

> "Money is based on PROMISES, which means so is "law". … Your money is only as good as your law, and your law is only as good as your money."
> — [Scott Duncan, Volume 13, 22 February 2016](https://thetenderforlaw.com/vol_xiii/462493170453287_948042318565034/#entry-948089958560270)

> "Fiat Money is DEBT IN TRANSIT. If you can buy good/services with it, it's MONEY OF EXCHANGE. The rest is MONEY OF ACCOUNT. MONEY OF EXCHANGE can buy MONEY OF ACCOUNT, but MONEY OF ACCOUNT cannot buy MONEY OF EXCHANGE."
> — [Scott Duncan, Volume 14, 23 April 2016](https://thetenderforlaw.com/vol_xiv/462493170453287_981483405220925/#entry-985202784848987)

> "SO: Banks ONLY function as a CURRENCY EXCHANGE."
> — [Scott Duncan, Volume 3, 3 July 2013](https://thetenderforlaw.com/vol_iii/462493170453287_532494196786517/#entry-532550620114208)

> "Currency is no good unless other people accept it."
> — [Scott Duncan, Volume 10, 21 June 2015](https://thetenderforlaw.com/vol_x/462493170453287_845066405529293/)

## Check yourself

> [!question]- You hold a $20 note. Using the two-sides habit, name the two sets of books it appears in, and on which side of each.
> Yours, as cash you have (an asset, the debit side). And the books of the bank that issued it, as a note it owes (a liability, the credit side).
