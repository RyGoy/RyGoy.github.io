## In Scott Duncan's words

He uses it in 12 posts, comments and articles. Where he says what it means, or uses it most plainly:

> But a TENDER is an UNCONDITIONAL GRANTING, not a contract. The law it tenders is a BENEFIT that you have obligations under. 
>
> You can't renegotiate a tender any more than you can renegotiate estoppel.
> — [Scott Duncan, Volume 1, 26 June 2013](https://thetenderforlaw.com/vol_i/462493170453287_462528253783112/#entry-529625503740053)

> TENDER is an unconditional offer of something.
> When I TENDER a resignation, it must be ACCEPTED.
>
> When they tender "LEGAL", then by using money, you have accepted the offer.
> — [Scott Duncan, Volume 3, 24 July 2013](https://thetenderforlaw.com/vol_iii/462493170453287_540523642650239/#entry-540663869302883)

> You still don't know what a TENDER FOR LAW is.
>
> It is an unconditional offer of the rule of law. That is all. The currency/accounting that follows means the tender is already accepted (or you are commiting fraud).
> — [Scott Duncan, Volume 4, 6 November 2013](https://thetenderforlaw.com/vol_iv/462493170453287_583552505014019/#entry-583788961657040)
