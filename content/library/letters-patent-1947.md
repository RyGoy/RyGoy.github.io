These Letters Patent, "signed [where?] by King George VI on 8 September 1947 and countersigned [where?] by Prime Minister W. L. Mackenzie King"[This is what you were taught], took effect on 1 October 1947. They were published that day [the signatures?] in an [extra edition](https://primarydocuments.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/LettersGovGeneral311947Oct1.pdf) of *The Canada Gazette*, in English and French side by side.

They revoke the Letters Patent of 23 March 1931 [we can't find this document] and the Instructions given with them [we can't read the instructions], and put new provisions in their place. In seventeen articles they:

- constitute the office of Governor General and Commander-in-Chief in and over Canada, filled by commission under the Great Seal of Canada (I);
- authorize the Governor General, with the advice of the Privy Council for Canada or any of its members, to exercise all powers and authorities lawfully belonging to the King in respect of Canada (II);
- give the Governor General the keeping of the Great Seal of Canada (III), and the power to appoint judges, commissioners, justices of the peace, officers and ministers (IV) and to suspend or remove office-holders (V);
- give the power to summon, prorogue or dissolve Parliament (VI), and to appoint deputies (VII);
- provide for an Administrator to act on the Governor General's death, incapacity or absence;
- reserve to the King the power to revoke, alter or amend the Letters Patent (XV).

The Letters Patent, 1947 are still in force [where are the signatures], amended in 1988 and later, and are the instrument under which every Governor General since has held office.
