*Weiss's Concise Trustee Handbook*, by Carlton Weiss, is a guide for the trustee of an express trust, which it describes as created under the original American common law and working within the general law-merchant. It sets out to give "clearly outlined methods and sample forms" for running the trust's affairs.

It opens with trust basics: a trust as property held by a trustee at the request of a settlor for the benefit of a beneficiary, and the express trust as one stated fully in words rather than inferred from conduct. It then covers the declaration of trust, the trust corpus, and the certificates of beneficial and capital interest the trustees may issue. The trustee's powers, duties, privileges and liabilities follow, with authorised representatives, and a comparison of the express trust with the corporation and other bodies that owe their existence to statute.

The second half is practical: commerce and doing business, limiting liability and risk, banking, transferring assets, issuing certificates and bonds, keeping minutes, legal actions for and against the trust, and dealing with the I.R.S. Its conclusion is that the way to thrive is to "own nothing and control everything." The last thirty pages are sample forms, from an asset sale agreement and assignments to an attorney retainer agreement.

Carlton Weiss also wrote [Those Who Mistrust](/articles/various-authors/those-who-mis-trust/), an article on the mistake of missing the point about express trusts.
