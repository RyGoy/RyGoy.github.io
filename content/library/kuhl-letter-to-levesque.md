Walter Frederick Kuhl (1905–1991) was a teacher and the Member of Parliament for Jasper—Edson, Alberta, from 1935 to 1949.[^wiki] Two weeks after the Parti Québécois won the Quebec election of 15 November 1976, he wrote to its leader, René Lévesque, from Spruce Grove, Alberta.[^letter]

He tells Lévesque that he was helped in his forty years of study by R. Rogers Smith, the author of [*Ho, Canada!*](/library/ho-canada/), and encloses his own House of Commons speeches and a pamphlet by Smith.[^letter] His argument, in ten points, is that the British North America Act made Canada a united colony, not a federal union; that the Statute of Westminster of 11 December 1931 passed sovereignty to the people and, through them, to the provinces; and that, since the provinces have signed no federal union since and the people have ratified no constitution, Quebec has no need of a referendum to separate. Every province is already a sovereign state. He suggests a proper federal constitution instead, in which the provinces would keep the powers they need.

### What we could and could not confirm

The copy here is a typed transcription, not a photograph of the signed letter, and we have found no archival original. The Myth is Canada says Lévesque went to Ottawa to ask Pierre Trudeau whether the letter was factual;[^myth] we have found no record of that meeting, or of any reply from Lévesque. His papers at the Bibliothèque et Archives nationales du Québec would be the place to look.

The letter was also printed in Kuhl's pamphlet *Canada: A Country Without a Constitution*, with a 1945 Commons speech on Smith's theories. The *Alberta Law Review* traces how these were reprinted in 1998 as *The Missing Key to Canada's Future* and taken up by "detaxers" as grounds to refuse income tax, and notes that courts have rebutted the argument, for example in *Butterfield v LeBlanc*, 2007 BCSC 235.[^alr] The pamphlet, [*Canada: A Country Without a Constitution*](https://www.themythiscanada.com/pdfs/Canada.A.Country.Without.A.Constitution.pdf), is on The Myth is Canada.
